SUMMARY
There is a plausible full proof with the stronger bound 5*C.size, hence ample
slack for the required 33*C.size. The crucial operation is reverse-mode
transposition of the circuit after retaining only degree zero and degree one
in line variables. This creates, with linear overhead, a circuit computing all
line coefficients as separate outputs. A C4-free multi-output lower bound then
applies. The whole argument uses global correctness on heavy point inputs.

1. MULTI-OUTPUT LEMMA

Let P be finite, let (B_l)_{l in L} be subsets of P with
  |B_l intersection B_m| <= 1 whenever l != m.
Consider a finite acyclic binary AND/OR circuit on variables (x_p)_{p in P},
with constants 0 and 1 permitted and arbitrary output wires h_l. Assume
  h_l(1_{P\B_l}) = 0.
For a wire v let
  S(v) = {p in P : h_v(1_{p}) = 1}.
Then
  sum_l |S(h_l)| <= number of OR gates + #{l : S(h_l) nonempty}.

Proof. Call a wire v l-compatible if h_v(1_{P\B_l})=0. Monotonicity gives
S(v) subset B_l for every compatible wire. Consequently a wire with at least
two singleton points can be compatible with at most one l.

Fix an output l with |S(h_l)|>=2. Recursively traverse backwards from h_l.
Stop at any wire with |S(v)|<=1, treating that wire as a boundary leaf labelled
by its singleton point, if any. At an OR gate v, both children are compatible;
keep both children. At an AND gate v, at least one child is compatible;
choose one such child and discard the other edge. The singleton relations are
  S(a OR b)=S(a) union S(b),
  S(a AND b)=S(a) intersection S(b).
Thus the chosen AND child contains all singleton points of its parent, and
has at least two points if the parent does. The construction is acyclic.
Every singleton in S(h_l) reaches a boundary singleton leaf. One may discard
empty boundary leaves; sharing does not hurt the following count.

The resulting finite backwards DAG is connected from its root. Its internal
OR gates have two outgoing edges (counting multiplicity), and its internal
AND gates one. With b binary vertices, u unary vertices and k distinct
boundary vertices, the total number of edges is 2b+u, while reachability of
all vertices gives at least b+u+k-1 edges. Hence k<=b+1. There are at least
|S(h_l)| distinct boundary singleton leaves, so |S(h_l)|-1<=b.

All internal gates in this traversal have at least two singleton points and
are l-compatible. Traversals belonging to different l therefore use disjoint
internal OR gates. Sum |S(h_l)|-1 over nonempty outputs; outputs of singleton
support add zero. This proves the lemma.

Constants cause no problem. Constant 0 has empty singleton support and stops.
Constant 1 is never compatible, so cannot occur in a selected traversal.
Repeated operands cause no problem: count edges with multiplicity, or remove
one repeated edge and strengthen the edge count.

Equivalent constructive view: each large-support compatible gate belongs to
at most one line, so choose its AND child once for that line, replace every
support-at-most-one wire by its singleton variable or 0, and retain OR gates.
This constructs disjoint-internal-gates OR DAGs covering S(h_l), with output
support contained in B_l. Their supports may grow, which is harmless.

2. REVERSE TRANSPOSED CIRCUIT

Write the original inputs as point variables X and line variables Y. Every
original wire v has a degree-zero-in-Y part a_v(X), obtained by setting all
Y=0, and a degree-one-in-Y part b_v(X,Y). They satisfy
  a_OR=a_u OR a_w,
  b_OR=b_u OR b_w,
  a_AND=a_u AND a_w,
  b_AND=(a_u AND b_w) OR (b_u AND a_w).
At a point input x_p, (a,b)=(x_p,0). At a line input y_l,
(a,b)=(0,y_l). These are precisely ReadK.linearParts under IsAO2.

The part b_out has the form OR_l (y_l AND g_l(X)). The g_l are computed
simultaneously by reverse mode:
  initialize every adjoint d_v=0, except d_out=1;
  process original gates in reverse order;
  for OR gate v=(u OR w):
    d_u := d_u OR d_v;
    d_w := d_w OR d_v;
  for AND gate v=(u AND w):
    d_u := d_u OR (d_v AND a_w);
    d_w := d_w OR (d_v AND a_u).
At the end g_l=d_{y_l}.

This is the standard transposition of a linear acyclic network over the
commutative Boolean semiring of point functions. A direct proof expands all
paths from y_l to the output: OR edges carry weight 1, an AND edge carries
the degree-zero value of the opposite operand, and the coefficient is the OR
over path products. Forward recurrence and reverse accumulation compute the
same path sum. Repeated operands give repeated contributions, harmless by OR
idempotence. No product can contain two line leaves since every edge weight
is a degree-zero function.

The d_v are changing references to wires, NOT overwritten gates. Each update
allocates an OR gate whose inputs are the old d_v and the new contribution.
Thus this is a genuine finite acyclic monotone circuit. Compute all a_v first
with <=s gates, then the d_v in reverse order. Each of the 2s operand positions
needs at most one new AND and one new OR. Total gates <=5s; OR gates <=3s.
Constants 0 and 1 are shared and free in the intermediate circuit. Its output
wires g_l may be aliases.

For exact constant bookkeeping:
  zero-line forward part: <=#original OR OR gates;
  reverse accumulations: <=2s OR gates;
  reverse products: <=2*#original AND AND gates;
so #OR(intermediate)<=3s, total(intermediate)<=5s.

3. WHY CORRECTNESS IMPLIES THE MULTI-OUTPUT HYPOTHESES

All proof trees counted by b_out are proof trees of C, so
  b_out(X,Y) <= C(X,Y)
pointwise. Equivalently, both components of linearParts are below the
original wire values, proved by gate induction.

Fix l and put Y=1_l, X=1_{P\B_l}, where B_l={p : I(p,l)}.
The incidence function is false on this assignment. Thus
  g_l(1_{P\B_l}) = 0.
This is the global/heavy-input fact that singleton/pair-only arguments lack.

For X=1_p, Y=1_l, the value b_out is by definition
  lineSingle C (2N) isLine p (N+l),
and is g_l(1_p). Monotonicity and the displayed compatibility imply that
g_l(1_p)=1 only for p in B_l. Thus the subtype incidence condition in the task
does not delete any singleton coefficient points, and its cardinal is exactly
sum_l |S(g_l)|.

The projective-plane unique-line axiom implies the intersection condition:
if two distinct p,p' belonged to both B_l and B_m, the uniqueness of their
common line would imply l=m. The line cardinal and N=q^2+q+1 axioms are not
needed for this particular combinatorial argument.

4. COUNT ACTIVE OUTPUTS

If a line input y_l is not named by any gate operand, its reverse adjoint is
zero unless the original circuit output is y_l itself. The latter is ruled
out by exact incidence correctness: on X=0,Y=1_l the circuit would be true
while the incidence function is false. Hence every l with S(g_l) nonempty is
named by at least one of the 2s operand positions. Therefore there are at most
2s active outputs. No expensive essential-input argument is required.

Original output outside all wires gives an identically-zero degree-one part
and the bound trivially. Original output a point input also gives a zero
degree-one part. A proof can split these easy cases before reverse mode, or
define the seed d_out only when out is a valid wire. The actual assigned
correctness and plane hypotheses exclude all input/missing-output cases,
but proving only exclusion of a line output is sufficient for counting.

5. CONCLUSION

Applying section 1 to the simultaneous coefficient circuit:
  #lineSingle incidence edges
    = sum_l |S(g_l)|
    <= #OR(intermediate) + #active line outputs
    <= 3s+2s = 5s <=33s.

Remaining work is formalization, not a missing combinatorial case. The most
useful potential reduction is a pair of independent propositions: a generic
multi-output C4-free coefficient bound, and a size-controlled transposition
statement expressing the g_l through the existing linearParts definition.

6. LEAN PLAN WITHOUT GRAPHS OR UNION-FIND

The request invariant is a direct refinement of Cnt/cnt_step in the already
checked Outside/L_meta_550.lean. It avoids all connected-component machinery.
A new working proof is being written in
  research/interactive_20261124/comb_lean2462/MultiOutput.lean
with imports Mathlib and PEqualNP.Defs only.

Represent each wire by a pair of finsets (S,T), where S is its true singleton
point support and T is the set of lines l whose heavy input P\B_l is rejected.
The default pair for a missing wire is (empty,univ), representing false.
For point input p the pair is ({p},{l:I p l}). Constants false and true have
pairs (empty,univ) and (univ,empty), respectively.

The gate algebra is especially simple:
  OR:  (S_a union S_b, T_a intersection T_b)
  AND: (S_a intersection S_b, T_a union T_b).
No separate monotonicity proof is needed: the invariant S x T subset I follows
directly from these equations and the input case. If |S|>=2, uniqueness of the
line through a pair of points implies |T|<=1.

For a list L of current wire entries, a request is (l,w), allowed when
l belongs to T(L[w]). Define
  footprint(L,W) = union_{(l,w) in W} {l} x S(L[w]).
The induction invariant is
  forall allowed finite W, |footprint(L,W)| <= t + |W|,
where t is the number of processed gates.

When appending entry e at position m=L.length:
  A. If |e.S|<=1, partition W into old requests (w!=m) and new requests (w=m).
     New requests contribute at most one labelled point apiece. Apply the
     induction hypothesis to old requests and sum the bounds.
  B. If |e.S|>=2, all allowed new requests have one common line label, hence
     W contains at most one such request r0=(l,m). For OR replace r0 by the
     at most two operand requests, both allowed; their supports cover e.S.
     For AND choose a child rejecting the heavy input. Its support contains
     e.S, so one allowed operand request suffices. The replacement request
     set has size at most |W|-1+2. Apply the induction hypothesis.
  C. If there are no new requests, the induction hypothesis applies unchanged.

This proves Count(t+1,L++[e]). At the end take one request (l,o_l) for every
line with nonempty singleton output support. Its footprint is exactly the
set of output singletons, and its cardinal is the number of active outputs.

7. FORMALIZING TRANSPOSITION VIA A FRONTIER PAIRING

For H1, do not formalize paths unless convenient. A simpler induction proves
the forward/reverse identity using a scalar-product invariant over a moving
wire frontier. Fix a point assignment X and a line assignment Y. Let a_w be
all zero-line wire values and b_w the one-line wire values, computed forward.
Let d_w be the current reverse adjoint references, evaluated in the constructed
point-only circuit D.

With k old wires not yet eliminated, maintain
  b_output = OR_{w<k} (d_w AND b_w).
Initially k=2N+s and d_output=1, all others zero, so this is immediate (if the
output wire is missing, b_output=0 and use all-zero adjoints).
At the next step w=k-1 is a gate with two operands u,v<w by ReadsBack.
Its b-value is either b_u OR b_v, or
  (a_u AND b_v) OR (b_u AND a_v).
Substitute this in the frontier term d_w AND b_w, distribute AND over OR, and
merge its contributions into existing d_u and d_v by OR. Then delete w from
the frontier. All other terms are unchanged. Repeated operands are handled
by two successive OR updates and Boolean idempotence; importantly, snapshot
d_w before updating either child. No child can equal w because of ReadsBack.

At the final frontier k=2N, b_point=0 and b_line_l=Y_l, so
  b_output(X,Y) = OR_l (d_line_l(X) AND Y_l).
Putting Y equal to a singleton extracts each coefficient exactly. The same
induction proves the identity for all Y, so no polynomial infrastructure is
needed. Updates to processed gate adjoints need not be overwritten by zero:
processed wires are simply omitted from the current frontier sum.

Concrete circuit state for this construction:
  (D : Circuit, aRef : Fin(2N+s)->Nat, dRef : Fin(2N+s)->Nat).
Indices are references to already allocated wires of D. Begin D with one
empty OR gate and one empty AND gate, so their wires are constant0 and1.
Map every original point input to itself and every original line input to
the constant0 wire; append one copied/renumbered gate for every original
gate to compute all aRef. Initialize all dRef to the constant0 reference,
except the output reference, set to constant1 when valid. Reverse elimination
appends at most2 AND products and exactly2 OR accumulations per original
gate. The arrays themselves add no gates.

Thus D has <=5s+2 gates. When s>=1 this is <=8s as required by the chosen H1.
For s=0, correctness rules out an actual input output; the original output
is missing, all line coefficients vanish, and one may choose D empty with
each o_l=N, an absent wire, giving size0 and no active outputs.

The number of active line outputs is bounded separately without an essential
inputs lemma: if line input y_l is absent from all2s operand positions, d_l
never receives an update. Its only possible nonzero source would be the
initial seed, i.e. output=y_l, which exact incidence correctness excludes
on X=0,Y={l}. So every active line admits an operand occurrence. Choose the
least such occurrence for an injection into Fin(2s), giving at most2s.
