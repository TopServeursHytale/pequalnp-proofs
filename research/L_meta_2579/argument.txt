NEW USEFUL LOCAL CONSEQUENCE: A SIDE IS PURE UP TO ONE EXCEPTION

Let j be any binary AND gate and u in Wmax(G,C,j). Put
  A = orIn(input0(j)), B = orIn(input1(j)).
For every a in A and b in B, the input with {u,a,b} switched on activates
j and x_u. Hence f_G is true on this three-point set, so
  Adj(u,a) OR Adj(u,b) OR Adj(a,b).
This uses a three-point/heavy input, not only edge inputs.

Remove the neighbors of u from the two sides:
  A0 = {a in A : not Adj(u,a)},
  B0 = {b in B : not Adj(u,b)}.
Then A0 x B0 is a complete bipartite subgraph of G. Since G has no4-cycle,
  |A0|<=1 OR |B0|<=1.
Indeed, if A0 has distinct a,a', every b in B0 is a common neighbor of a,a',
and NoFourCycle bounds the number of such b by1.

Thus every junk AND in a leaf context has a linear-input side which is
contained in N(u) except for at most one vertex. This is stronger than the
bare singleton-support condition. It suggests a descent which charges the
exception or the first OR where the side becomes large; fully pure branching
with a singleton operand is exactly the certificate already removed from R.

The remaining obstacle is GLOBAL sharing: the exceptional side and its leaf
context u can vary between different top consumers of the same junk gate.
One cannot sum a per-gate or per-context bound without controlling this reuse.
Case B is a different obstruction: products of two junk functions can be below
f_G even though neither function has an exposed leaf context. Nested/shared
junk gates need to be addressed there. These are mathematically different
children of the proposed structural cut.

SECOND LOCAL DICHOTOMY: MANY LEAF CONTEXTS FORCE A COMMON BAD VERTEX

Suppose a gate j accepts a non-edge pair {a,b}. For every u in Wmax(j),
activation on {u,a,b} forces u adjacent to a or b. Thus
  Wmax(j) subset N(a) union N(b).
If j also accepts a disjoint non-edge pair {c,d}, then
  Wmax(j) subset (N(a) union N(b)) intersection (N(c) union N(d)).
The right-hand set is the union of four common-neighbor sets. Each has at
most one vertex by NoFourCycle, since the two pairs are disjoint. Therefore
  |Wmax(j)| <= 4.

Consequently, a gate with at least5 leaf contexts in Wmax cannot have two
vertex-disjoint bad pair witnesses. Its bad pair witnesses form a pairwise
intersecting family of two-element sets. Such a family is either a star or
contained in a triangle. A triangle of bad pairs {a,b},{b,c},{a,c} forces
Wmax into the union of three common-neighbor sets and hence |Wmax|<=3.
So, when |Wmax|>=5, all bad two-element witnesses share a common vertex.

Singleton bad witnesses fit the same conclusion: if j accepts singleton a,
then Wmax(j) subset N(a). Any bad pair disjoint from a would bound Wmax by2.
Therefore all bad witnesses of size<=2 share a common vertex when |Wmax|>=5.

For a bottommost junk gate in the InCone order, TopForm excludes any below-F
AND descendant, while bottommost-junk excludes all other AND descendants.
Its operands are therefore ORs of input variables and all its prime implicants
have size<=2. In that restricted situation the common-bad-vertex property
upgrades to the global implication gate_j <= f_G OR x_r. It may provide a
useful local simplification for a future induction on nested/shared junk.
The actual cost-preserving surgery has NOT been proved here, and no global
linear bound follows merely by summing these local statements.

THIRD LOCAL LEMMA: COUNTING ALMOST-PURE BRANCHES

Fix an OR gate h and an operand position whose orIn is the singleton {v}.
Let S=orIn(h), and assume |S|>=3. Consider centers u for which uv is an edge
and S has at most one point outside N(u). When v belongs to S (as it does for
a valid earlier operand in AO2), choose distinct a,b in S\{v}. The center u
must be adjacent to at least one of a,b, because it has at most one exception.
Since it is also adjacent to v, it belongs to
  (N(v) intersection N(a)) union (N(v) intersection N(b)).
Each intersection has cardinal at most1 by NoFourCycle. Thus there are at most
two centers for a fixed gate and operand position. Consequently all edges
with such an almost-pure branching witness can be bounded by4*C.size.

The analogous statement with at most k exceptions and |S|>=k+2 has at most
k+1 centers per port: choose k+1 points of S\{v}; some one must be adjacent
to u, and each point supplies at most one common neighbor with v. The pure
branching count in the parent reduction is precisely the k=0 case.

Without requiring the adjacency uv or the fact v belongs to S, an almost-pure
set S of size at least3 admits at most3 centers, by choosing a triple in S
and covering all centers by its three pairwise common-neighbor sets. This
weaker formulation gives6*C.size and does not need the operand-validity step.
